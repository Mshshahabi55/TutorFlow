# ADR-021: Learning Plans, Enrollment & Settlement Architecture

## Status

**Proposed — 2026-07-27.** No section of this ADR is Accepted. It is written entirely at the owner's own direction, in response to a request ("RC4.1") to replace pay-per-session with a Learning Plan/Enrollment commerce model, after the owner was shown that request conflicts with standing scope documents and chose, from four offered paths, to have this design produced first, with no code, for review and explicit acceptance before any implementation begins.

## Governance Note (read first)

This ADR surfaces **four** separate conflicts between the requested business model and currently-approved documents. None is resolved by this ADR; each is recorded as a Question Requiring Approval (below) and none may be silently decided by implementation:

1. **Payments remain out of v1 scope** — unchanged since `ADR-020`/`docs/phases/PHASE-05A-REPORT.md` first surfaced this (`PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md` Project Scope; `PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md` Section 9; `ARCHITECTURE.md` line 60). Everything in this ADR is downstream of that same unresolved scope question.
2. **Recurring/standing bookings remain out of v1 scope** — `PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md` SCH-4 (Must priority): *"Recurring/standing bookings are out of scope for v1. Only discrete, single-session bookings are supported... no option to create a recurring series is presented."* A Learning Plan's core premise — "Sessions Per Week," "Choose Weekly Schedule," automatic generation of many future Sessions from one purchase — **is** a recurring-booking capability by any reasonable reading of SCH-4. This is a new conflict, not previously surfaced by `ADR-020` (which was scoped to a single Session's payment, not a series). Section "Cross-Context Integration" below proposes a way to satisfy the *business* request without introducing a new recurring-*availability* Domain concept, but the resulting capability is still, functionally, a recurring booking — the owner must decide whether that satisfies SCH-4's intent or requires SCH-4 itself to be revisited.
3. **`ADR-002`'s four bounded contexts do not include Payments, Enrollment, or Billing** — and its own Future Evolution section anticipates exactly this: *"Any new bounded context... would be introduced through a new ADR that extends or supersedes this one, not by informally attaching it to an existing context."* This ADR proposes such an extension (a fifth context) — see below.
4. **RC4.1's requested future-provider roster names Stripe.** `ADR-018` Decision Point 7 (Accepted) forbids any dependency on Stripe "for any capability," and Decision Point 5 requires an Iranian PSP. This ADR resolves that conflict in favor of the higher-precedence, Accepted `ADR-018` per `CLAUDE.md`'s own authority-precedence rule, and drops Stripe from the roster — flagged here rather than silently omitted, since RC4.1's literal text named it.

**Before any implementation phase referenced in this ADR may begin, the owner must resolve Questions 1–4 above through the Constitution's own Decision-Making Process** — the same posture `ADR-020` already took, now extended to the two new conflicts (2) and (3) this broader model surfaces.

## Context

`ADR-020` (Proposed, 2026-07-22) designed a `Payment` aggregate referencing a single `SessionId`, paid in full at the moment of booking one lesson — "pay per session." The current request ("RC4.1") replaces that premise entirely: a Student (or Parent) purchases a **Learning Plan** — a bundle of N future Sessions at a fixed cadence over a fixed duration — and payment creates an **Enrollment**, not a Session. Sessions are then generated automatically from the Enrollment. This is a materially different shape from `ADR-020`'s design, not an incremental extension of it (see "Relationship to ADR-020," below).

The existing Scheduling & Booking model (`docs/phases/PHASE-05A-REPORT.md` Section 3, restated by `ADR-020`) is unchanged by this request: `Session` and `AvailabilitySlot` remain two separate, ratified aggregate roots (`ADR-015`); `Session.Book(...)` is `internal`, reachable only through `AvailabilitySlot.Book(...)`; the unique index on `Sessions.AvailabilitySlotId` is unconditional (`ADR-014`); a Tutor declares availability one concrete `AvailabilitySlot` at a time; nothing in Scheduling & Booking today has any notion of price, a plan, or a purchase.

## Problem Statement

Given a request to replace pay-per-session with a Learning-Plan/Enrollment commerce model — while the existing Scheduling & Booking aggregate boundary, concurrency mechanism, and audit mechanism are all already Accepted and must not be silently revised, and while payments and recurring bookings both remain formally out of v1 scope — what Domain aggregates, bounded-context placement, cross-context integration shape, and CQRS/API/frontend design would this model require, such that the owner can evaluate and either accept or amend it before any code is written?

## Relationship to ADR-020

**This ADR supersedes `ADR-020` in full**, rather than extending it, because the fundamental unit of commerce changes: `ADR-020`'s `Payment` references exactly one `SessionId` and is created at booking time, one per lesson; this ADR's `EnrollmentPayment` references exactly one `EnrollmentId` and is created at plan-purchase time, before any Session exists. Keeping both designs alive in parallel would leave two competing, contradictory "how does money enter this system" answers on record. Everything in `ADR-020` that does not depend on the per-Session premise is carried forward unchanged, by explicit reuse, not re-derivation:

- The **`IPaymentGateway` port shape** (`InitiateAsync`/`VerifyAsync`, Application-layer interface, `MockPaymentGateway` Infrastructure adapter, Development/Testing-only DI guard) — reused verbatim, only the request/result payloads' owning aggregate changes (`EnrollmentId`/`EnrollmentPayment` in place of `SessionId`/`Payment`).
- The **idempotency design** (our own id as primary lookup key embedded in the callback URL; provider's opaque reference as secondary cross-check; a status check on the aggregate's own state machine, not a new table) — reused verbatim.
- The **amount-fixing discipline** (fixed once at initiation, never re-derived from a later, possibly-changed source fact) — reused, with the "possibly-changed source fact" now being `LearningPlan.Price` (which an Admin could edit) rather than `Tutor.HourlyRate`.
- The **filtered-unique-index concurrency pattern** for "at most one live payment attempt" (`ADR-014`'s mechanism, reused rather than a new invention).
- The **Domain-Error-vs-Infrastructure-Failure split** for gateway outcomes (`ADR-008`), unchanged.

What does **not** carry forward: `ADR-020`'s Slot-Reservation Problem (Options A/B/C) was scoped to *one* slot for *one* Session held during *one* payment's verification window. This ADR's problem is structurally different — reserving a **set** of future Sessions across a plan's entire duration, atomically, as a consequence of one payment — and is addressed fresh below, not by selecting one of `ADR-020`'s three options.

## Decision — New Bounded Context: Enrollment & Billing (AWAITING OWNER DECISION)

Per `ADR-002`'s own Future Evolution clause, a new bounded context requires its own ADR, not an informal attachment to an existing one. This ADR proposes: **Enrollment & Billing**, a fifth bounded context, owning `LearningPlan`, `Enrollment`, `EnrollmentPayment`, `RefundRequest`, `SettlementLedgerEntry`, `SettlementBatch`, and `Invoice` (their value objects and states below). This mirrors, not reinvents, `ADR-020`'s already-proposed "Payments" context — this ADR folds that proposal into a broader context because Enrollment, not a bare Payment, is now the aggregate Sessions are generated from, and Payment/Refund/Settlement/Invoice are all facts *about* an Enrollment, not independent concerns needing their own contexts.

**New upstream/downstream edge, not present in `ADR-002`'s dependency graph today:** Enrollment & Billing must be able to **invoke** Scheduling & Booking's own use cases (to generate Sessions after a successful payment) and **read** Identity & Relationship (Tutor identity/approval, Student/Parent identity, Subject) and Scheduling & Booking (AvailabilitySlot availability, to validate a chosen weekly schedule before accepting payment). This is a new direction: today, only Identity & Relationship is upstream of Scheduling & Booking; Discovery and Marketplace Oversight are downstream leaves that never trigger a Scheduling & Booking *write* from their own workflow. Enrollment & Billing would be the first context whose own use case (verify payment) needs to **cause** a write in another context's owned aggregate (create N Sessions) — through that context's own interface, never direct data access, per `ADR-002`'s Integration Rules, which this ADR does not propose relaxing. This is flagged as a genuinely new integration shape requiring the same explicit ratification `ADR-002`/`ADR-015` themselves required, not a routine addition.

## Proposal — Aggregates

Full method signatures are intentionally omitted (design-only phase); each aggregate below states its fields, state machine, and invariants at the level `ADR-020` used for `Payment`.

### LearningPlan

Fields: `Name`, `Subject` (reuses the existing Subject value object from Identity & Relationship, by value — not a foreign key — so a plan can be filtered against a chosen Tutor's own Subject at booking time), `DurationInDays`, `TotalSessions`, `SessionsPerWeek`, `SessionDuration` (reuses the existing Session Duration value object), `Type` (`Individual` | `Group` — see Non-Goals: this ADR designs `Individual` only), `Price` (whole Rial, per Currency & Money below), `IsActive`, `Description`. Owned and authored by Admin/Staff only — no Tutor-authoring capability is proposed, consistent with RC4.1's own capability list (Admin manages Learning Plans; Tutor is not listed as able to). **Open product question, not resolved here:** whether a `LearningPlan` is a single platform-wide catalog entry usable with *any* Tutor whose `Subject` matches, or must additionally be scoped to specific Tutors — RC4.1's own flow ("Find Tutor → Choose Learning Plan") reads as the former; this ADR recommends the former (Subject-matching only, no per-Tutor plan authoring) as the minimal design consistent with what was actually requested, but lists it as a Question Requiring Approval.

**Invariants:** `TotalSessions = SessionsPerWeek × ceil(DurationInDays / 7)` is not enforced by this ADR as a hard equation (the four worked examples in RC4.1's own request — e.g., "8 Sessions / 2 Sessions/Week" over 30 days — are internally consistent under it, but an Admin should not be blocked from authoring an intentionally uneven cadence); recommended as a soft validation warning, not a Domain invariant, pending owner confirmation. `Price` is never negative or zero for an `Active` plan.

**No state machine beyond `IsActive`/inactive** — a plan is not itself a workflow; `IsActive = false` (never a hard delete) prevents new Enrollments from referencing it while leaving existing Enrollments' historical reference intact (an Enrollment must never lose the plan it was purchased against, mirroring why `Payment.Amount` is fixed at initiation rather than re-read live).

### Enrollment

Fields: `StudentId`, `ParentGuardianId` (nullable — present only when a Parent purchased on the Student's behalf, mirroring how `Session` already carries both `TutorId`/`StudentId` by identity reference only, per `ADR-002`), `TutorId`, `LearningPlanId`, `PriceAtPurchase` (fixed at creation, never re-read from a possibly-later-edited `LearningPlan.Price` — the same discipline `ADR-020` established for `Payment.Amount`), `Status`, `StartDate`, `EndDate` (both computed once from `LearningPlan.DurationInDays` at the moment the Enrollment becomes `Active`, not re-derived later), `RemainingSessions`, `CompletedSessions`, `CancelledSessions`.

**States:** `PendingPayment → Active → Completed | Paused | Cancelled | Expired` (as RC4.1 requested, adopted verbatim rather than reconciled against `ADR-020`'s different, now-superseded `Payment` states, since these two state machines govern different aggregates).

- `PendingPayment` — created the instant a Student/Parent commits to a plan+Tutor+weekly-schedule combination and an `EnrollmentPayment` is initiated. No Session yet exists. Carries no `RemainingSessions` figure yet (undefined until Active).
- `Active` — reached only when its initiating `EnrollmentPayment` reaches `Succeeded` **and** Session generation (below) completes successfully. `RemainingSessions = TotalSessions`, decremented as each generated Session is `Completed`, `Cancelled`, or `NoShow`'d (mirrored, not duplicated: Scheduling & Booking still owns `Session.Status`; Enrollment's counters are updated by Enrollment & Billing's own handler reacting to a Session-lifecycle Domain Event read through Scheduling & Booking's interface, per `ADR-002`'s Integration Rules — never by Enrollment & Billing writing to `Session` directly).
- `Completed` — reached when `RemainingSessions` reaches zero via natural completion (not cancellation).
- `Paused` — an explicit, Admin- or Tutor-initiated hold (RC4.1 names this state but does not specify who may invoke it or what happens to already-generated future Sessions during a pause — Question Requiring Approval).
- `Cancelled` — an explicit termination before `Completed`, the trigger for `RefundRequest` eligibility (below). What happens to already-generated, not-yet-occurred future Sessions on Cancellation (auto-cancel each remaining Session vs. leave them and let the Tutor/Admin clean up) is not specified by RC4.1 — Question Requiring Approval.
- `Expired` — `EndDate` passed with `RemainingSessions > 0` and no natural completion — RC4.1 lists this state but does not specify whether it is terminal-only or eligible for extension/renewal. This ADR recommends treating "Renew Enrollment" (an RC4.1-requested Student capability) as **creating a new `Enrollment`** referencing the same `LearningPlan`/Tutor, rather than mutating a `Completed`/`Expired` one back to `Active` — consistent with `Enrollment`'s `StartDate`/`EndDate`/`PriceAtPurchase` being fixed-at-creation facts about one purchase, not a mutable subscription. Flagged as a recommendation, not a decision.

**Invariants:** `RemainingSessions + CompletedSessions + CancelledSessions = TotalSessions` at all times once `Active`. At most one `EnrollmentPayment` may reach `Succeeded` for a given `Enrollment` (enforced the same way `ADR-020` enforced "at most one live Payment per Session" — a filtered unique index, here `WHERE Status = Succeeded`, reused verbatim from `ADR-014`'s pattern).

### EnrollmentPayment

Renamed from `ADR-020`'s `Payment` to avoid implying it is still keyed to a Session. Fields: `EnrollmentId`, `Amount` (whole Rial, fixed at `Initiate`, equal to `Enrollment.PriceAtPurchase` at that instant), `GatewayReferenceId`, `Status`.

**States (adopted from RC4.1's own naming, a superset of `ADR-020`'s three):** `Pending → Succeeded | Failed | Cancelled | Expired`; `Succeeded → Refunded` (reached only as the terminal effect of an **Approved** `RefundRequest`, below — `EnrollmentPayment` itself exposes no direct "refund" method; the refund workflow lives entirely on `RefundRequest`, consistent with RC4.1's explicit instruction that refunds belong to the Enrollment, not the payment or the session). `Cancelled` (new relative to `ADR-020`) covers a user-initiated abandonment at the gateway before any explicit failure or timeout — distinguished from `Expired` (no callback ever arrived) the same way `ADR-020` already distinguished `Failed` from `Expired`.

**Invariants, idempotency, and amount-fixing:** identical in kind to `ADR-020`'s `Payment` (see "Relationship to ADR-020" above) — not re-derived here.

Multiple `EnrollmentPayment` rows may exist against one `Enrollment` while it remains `PendingPayment` (a failed/expired/cancelled attempt does not preclude retrying), mirroring `ADR-020`'s "no resurrection, a retry is a new row" rule. Once one reaches `Succeeded`, no further `EnrollmentPayment` may be initiated against that `Enrollment`.

### RefundRequest

A new, independent aggregate root — not a child record on `EnrollmentPayment` as `ADR-020` modeled refunds, because RC4.1 explicitly requires refunds to belong to `Enrollment` and to carry their own approval workflow (Requested → Approved/Rejected → Refunded), a genuine multi-step process with its own actor (who requested, who approved/rejected), unlike `ADR-020`'s single-step "Admin records that a refund occurred" fact. This is consistent with the same small-aggregate-per-invariant heuristic `ADR-015`/`ADR-020` already established: a workflow with its own state machine and its own actors is a root, not a fact folded into another aggregate.

Fields: `EnrollmentId`, `RequestedByAccountId`, `RequestedAmount`, `ApprovedAmount` (nullable until Approved; may be less than requested — proration), `Status` (`Requested → Approved | Rejected`; `Approved → Refunded`), `ReviewedByAccountId`, `Reason`.

**Invariants:** `RequestedAmount` (and, once set, `ApprovedAmount`) never exceeds the sum of `Succeeded` `EnrollmentPayment.Amount` for the referenced `Enrollment`, less any already-`Refunded` amount. **Not decided by this ADR:** the proration policy for a mid-plan cancellation (e.g., is a refund pro-rated by `RemainingSessions / TotalSessions`, by elapsed time, or a flat Admin judgment call?) — RC4.1 does not specify one, and this ADR does not invent one; recommended default is "Admin sets `ApprovedAmount` manually per request, no automatic formula," the option requiring zero new business rule, until an owner decision names a formula.

**On `Approved` → execution:** per `ADR-020`'s already-established governance posture ("Refunds are recorded, not executed... no gateway refund API call exists in v1"), `RefundRequest.Refunded` is reached the same way — an Admin records that a refund occurred (by some out-of-band mechanism), not through a real gateway refund call. This ADR does not revisit that posture; it is restated because RC4.1's request does not itself say whether refund execution is real or recorded, and `ADR-020`'s already-established answer applies unless the owner says otherwise.

### Settlement — `SettlementLedgerEntry` and `SettlementBatch`

RC4.1 asks for "architecture only, no real payout execution" — consistent with `ADR-020`'s own Non-Goal ("Payouts to Tutors... out of MVP scope"), so this section designs a ledger, not a payment-execution mechanism.

**`SettlementLedgerEntry`** — one immutable row created per completed lesson: `TutorId`, `SessionId`, `EnrollmentId`, `Amount` (the Tutor's earned portion of that Session — see below), `CreatedAtUtc`, `SettlementBatchId` (nullable until included in a batch). Created reactively, when Enrollment & Billing observes a `SessionCompleted` Domain Event (read through Scheduling & Booking's interface, same integration pattern as Enrollment's own session counters above) — never by mutating a single running "balance" field, which would reintroduce exactly the check-then-act concurrency hazard `ADR-014` was written to eliminate for `AvailabilitySlot.IsConsumed`. A Tutor's/Admin's "current balance" is a **derived read** (`SUM(Amount) WHERE SettlementBatchId IS NULL`), not a stored, independently-mutable figure — this avoids inventing a second concurrency-control problem alongside the one `ADR-014` already solved.

**Open question this ADR does not resolve:** what fraction of `Enrollment.PriceAtPurchase` a single `SettlementLedgerEntry.Amount` represents — `Enrollment.PriceAtPurchase / TotalSessions` (even split) is the simplest rule and is recommended, but RC4.1 does not specify a platform-commission deduction (`BUSINESS_MODEL.md` Open Question 5 — "will the platform apply a commission" — is itself still an open question, unresolved by any approved document). This ADR recommends `SettlementLedgerEntry.Amount = Enrollment.PriceAtPurchase / TotalSessions` (full pass-through, zero commission) as the only rule that invents no new, undocumented business fact — a platform commission, if introduced later, is a separate, explicit future decision (mirroring `ADR-020`'s own treatment of commission as out-of-scope-but-named-for-forward-compatibility), not something this ADR should default to a nonzero value for.

**`SettlementBatch`** — created by an explicit Admin action ("settle now" for a given Tutor, or a set of Tutors): references a set of previously-unbatched `SettlementLedgerEntry` rows, stamps them with its own id, and itself carries `Status` (`Pending → Settled`) and `SettledAtUtc`. "Settled" records that the Admin has, by some out-of-band means, actually paid the Tutor — identical in spirit to `RefundRequest.Refunded` and `ADR-020`'s original refund posture: the fact is recorded, the payout mechanism is not built.

### Invoice

A derived, immutable read record, not a workflow aggregate — generated once, at the moment an `EnrollmentPayment` reaches `Succeeded`, as a snapshot of `Enrollment`, `LearningPlan` (name, duration, session count), `PriceAtPurchase`, and placeholder `Tax`/`Discount` fields (RC4.1 asks for these to be "future-ready" — modeled as always-zero fields with a named seam, mirroring `ADR-019`'s "named but single-valued" pattern for Currency, rather than left absent) at that instant. No PDF/document generation is designed or implied — "View Invoice" is a read of this snapshot's fields, formatting deferred entirely to Presentation.

## Proposal — Cross-Context Integration: Enrollment-Triggered Session Generation

This is the hardest new problem this ADR surfaces — RC4.1's own request ("Payment → Enrollment Created → Availability Reserved → Sessions Generated → Tutor Calendar Updated") implies a single, automatic, atomic-feeling pipeline. Two already-Accepted rules make a literal single-transaction reading of that pipeline impossible without revision:

1. **`PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md` SCH-4** excludes recurring/standing bookings from v1 (Governance Note, Item 2).
2. **`ADR-004`'s Transaction Boundaries** (Accepted, restated by `ADR-014`/`ADR-015`/`ADR-016`): *"A transaction boundary never spans the owned aggregates of two different bounded contexts at once... A use case needing effects in two contexts... is expressed as separate transactions, one per owning context, coordinated by the consuming context's Application layer — never as a single cross-context transaction."*

**Recommended resolution to (1) — no new recurring-availability Domain concept.** A Tutor continues declaring availability exactly as today: one concrete `AvailabilitySlot` per instance, no new recurring-pattern aggregate. What changes is purely a **demand-side UX convenience**: at Enrollment checkout, "Choose Weekly Schedule" means the Student selects a day-of-week+time pattern (e.g., "Tuesdays & Thursdays, 17:00"), and the Application layer resolves that pattern against the Tutor's **already-declared**, individually-concrete `AvailabilitySlot` rows matching it, for as many future occurrences as `LearningPlan.TotalSessions` requires. Each resulting Session is booked against a real, individually-declared slot, through the existing `AvailabilitySlot.Book(...)` method, unchanged — no new booking primitive, no new `SessionStatus`, no new invariant in Scheduling & Booking at all. Whether this reading satisfies SCH-4's intent (since the *demand-side outcome* — many Sessions from one purchase, with no further per-Session confirmation — is functionally a recurring booking, however it is technically assembled) is not this ADR's call to make; it is listed as Question Requiring Approval 2, restated from the Governance Note.

**Consequence of the above, itself a new business rule requiring approval:** if the Tutor has not pre-declared enough matching `AvailabilitySlot` rows to cover the plan's full duration at checkout time, this ADR recommends the checkout wizard **block before payment** — the weekly-schedule step validates live availability (reading Scheduling & Booking through its own interface, a pure read, no different in kind from Discovery's existing read-only composition) and refuses to proceed to payment unless all N required slots currently exist and are open. This avoids ever needing partial fulfillment, a "some sessions missing" Enrollment state, or refund-for-missing-sessions logic — but it does mean a Tutor with sparse availability cannot be enrolled against a plan requiring more sessions than they've declared, which may be a real product friction RC4.1 did not anticipate. Recommended as the only option requiring no new mid-plan-remediation Domain concept; not decided.

**Resolution to (2) — two transactions, per `ADR-004`'s own already-established pattern, not a new invention.** `ADR-004`'s Transaction Boundaries section already names exactly this shape for a cross-context use case: separate transactions, one per owning context, coordinated by the consuming context's Application layer. Applied here:

- **Transaction 1 (Enrollment & Billing's own):** the payment-verification handler validates the gateway's callback, marks `EnrollmentPayment.Succeeded`, and — *only if* the live-availability check (above) still holds at this instant — marks `Enrollment.Active`. Commits or rolls back as one unit, exactly like every existing single-context handler in this codebase.
- **Transaction 2 (Scheduling & Booking's own), immediately following, coordinated by Enrollment & Billing's Application layer:** Enrollment & Billing invokes a new Scheduling & Booking Application-layer use case (through Scheduling & Booking's own interface — never direct construction of `Session`/`AvailabilitySlot` by Enrollment & Billing code, per `ADR-002`'s Integration Rules) that, in one transaction, calls `AvailabilitySlot.Book(...)` N times — once per resolved slot — each producing a `Session` tagged with the new `EnrollmentId` field (see Migration Strategy). This transaction is itself all-or-nothing within Scheduling & Booking (if any one of the N slots was consumed by an unrelated race between the checkout-time validation and this moment, the whole batch rejects rather than partially booking) — the same "at most one success, explicit rejection, never partial" posture CONST-1 already requires of a single booking, extended to a batch.

**The resulting gap, named rather than hidden:** between Transaction 1 committing (`Enrollment.Active`, money captured) and Transaction 2 committing (Sessions actually exist), there is a real, normally sub-second window where an `Enrollment` is `Active` with no visible Sessions yet — and a real, if rare, failure mode where Transaction 2 rejects *after* Transaction 1 already committed (the race case above). This ADR does not invent a silent auto-retry or a silent auto-refund for that failure mode — both are undocumented new business rules. It recommends: Transaction 2's failure is surfaced as an Admin-visible, explicitly-flagged "Enrollment active, session generation incomplete" state (not a new formal `Enrollment.Status` value — a supplementary flag/queue item, mirroring `ADM-4`'s already-existing, if unimplemented, "resolve booking conflicts" admin-tooling gap noted in `docs/phases/PHASE-05A-REPORT.md`), for manual resolution rather than an automatic decision this ADR would otherwise be inventing. Listed as a Question Requiring Approval.

## Currency & Money

`LearningPlan.Price`, `Enrollment.PriceAtPurchase`, `EnrollmentPayment.Amount`, `RefundRequest.RequestedAmount`/`ApprovedAmount`, and `SettlementLedgerEntry.Amount` are all whole-Rial figures, displayed in Toman, converted only through the existing shared money module (`frontend/src/shared/money/` per `CLAUDE.md`'s standing rule) — never inline, never a new conversion path. "Currency" on `LearningPlan` is modeled as a named-but-single-valued fact, the identical pattern `ADR-019` already established for `HourlyRate` and `ADR-020` already reused for `Payment.Amount`: a real, distinct field exists (for the same v2-forward-compatibility reason `ADR-019` gave), but v1 populates and enforces exactly one value (IRR) and rejects construction with any other. This is not a new decision; it is `ADR-019`'s existing decision applied to a new field, restated here so `LearningPlan`'s "Currency" field (as RC4.1 literally requested it) does not read as reopening `ADR-019`'s "no multi-currency in v1" decision.

## Payment Provider Port

Reused from `ADR-020` verbatim in shape (see "Relationship to ADR-020"): `IPaymentGateway` (Application layer), `MockPaymentGateway` (Infrastructure, Development/Testing-only). This ADR does not rename it to RC4.1's suggested `IPaymentProvider` — no functional difference exists between the two names, and reusing `ADR-020`'s already-proposed name avoids a gratuitous rename of a not-yet-implemented interface. Future adapters: **ZarinPal, Zibal, Pay.ir, NextPay** — the four RC4.1-requested names consistent with `ADR-018`'s Iranian-PSP requirement. **Stripe is dropped from the roster** per the Governance Note's Item 4 — `ADR-018` Decision Point 7 forbids it outright, and no future-provider list in this codebase may include it regardless of what any single feature request asks for.

## Domain Events & Audit Trail

New events, all past-tense per `DOMAIN_MODEL.md`'s existing convention, all governance-relevant (money and enrollment state changing) and therefore required additions to `AuditDomainEventHandler`'s explicit `switch` per `CLAUDE.md`'s own non-negotiable rule — restating, not reopening, the same obligation `ADR-020` already flagged for its five payment events:

`LearningPlanCreated`, `LearningPlanDeactivated`, `EnrollmentPaymentInitiated`, `EnrollmentPaymentSucceeded`, `EnrollmentPaymentFailed`, `EnrollmentPaymentExpired`, `EnrollmentActivated`, `EnrollmentSessionsGenerated` (or its partial-failure counterpart, `EnrollmentSessionGenerationFailed`, per the gap named above), `EnrollmentCancelled`, `EnrollmentExpired`, `RefundRequested`, `RefundApproved`, `RefundRejected`, `RefundExecuted`, `SettlementBatchCreated`, `SettlementBatchSettled`.

This is a materially larger addition to the audit `switch` than `ADR-020`'s five — recommended in full, consistent with `ADR-020`'s own reasoning ("money changing hands is squarely the kind of governance-relevant fact CLAUDE.md's own non-negotiable rule... exist[s] to capture"), extended here to enrollment-state changes for the same reason.

## CQRS Shape (design-level — no handlers written)

**Commands:** `CreateLearningPlan`, `UpdateLearningPlan`, `DeactivateLearningPlan`, `ValidateWeeklySchedule` (the pre-payment live-availability check, a query in effect but listed here since it gates a command), `InitiateEnrollment` (creates `Enrollment` in `PendingPayment` + first `EnrollmentPayment` in `Pending`), `VerifyEnrollmentPayment` (the gateway callback handler — Transaction 1 above), `RequestRefund`, `ApproveRefund`, `RejectRefund`, `RecordRefundExecuted`, `PauseEnrollment`, `ResumeEnrollment`, `CancelEnrollment`, `CreateSettlementBatch`, `MarkSettlementBatchSettled`.

**Queries:** `GetLearningPlans` (filterable by Subject/Active), `GetLearningPlanById`, `GetEnrollmentById`, `GetMyEnrollments` (Student/Parent — Parent's includes children's), `GetEnrollmentSessions`, `GetTutorAssignedEnrollments`, `GetPaymentHistory`, `GetInvoice`, `GetRefundRequests` (Admin queue), `GetSettlementQueue` (Admin, per-Tutor unbatched balance), `GetRevenueSummary` (Admin).

## API Shape (design-level — no endpoints implemented)

Illustrative only, not a committed contract: `GET/POST /learning-plans`, `PATCH /learning-plans/{id}`, `POST /learning-plans/{id}/deactivate`, `POST /enrollments` (initiate), `POST /enrollments/{id}/payment-callback`, `GET /enrollments/{id}`, `GET /enrollments/mine`, `POST /enrollments/{id}/cancel`, `POST /enrollments/{id}/refund-requests`, `POST /refund-requests/{id}/approve`, `POST /refund-requests/{id}/reject`, `GET /settlements/queue`, `POST /settlements/batches`, `POST /settlements/batches/{id}/settle`. **Every one of these, if implemented, requires its own non-Open row in `docs/api/AUTHORIZATION_MATRIX.md` before being considered done** — restating `CLAUDE.md`'s own Definition of Done, not a new rule.

## Frontend Implications (design-level — no components written)

The booking wizard's replacement (Choose Tutor → Choose Learning Plan → Choose Weekly Schedule → Review → Payment) is itself a public-flow change (`CLAUDE.md` Stop Condition: "would change the public API contract... a renamed route, a changed response shape for an existing endpoint") and is not designed further here beyond naming its steps, consistent with this being a backend/Domain-first ADR. A Checkout page (Tutor, Plan, Duration, Total Sessions, Weekly Schedule, Price, Tax/Discount placeholders, Final Price, Secure Payment button) and per-role views (Admin: Learning Plans/Enrollments/Payments/Refunds/Settlement Queue/Revenue; Student/Parent: Browse/Purchase/Active Enrollment/Remaining Sessions/Renew/History/Invoices; Tutor: Assigned Enrollments/Students/Upcoming Sessions/Settlement Balance, no payment management) all reuse this repo's existing marketplace-card design system (RC1–RC3.3) rather than introducing new visual language — no new pattern is proposed here.

## Migration Strategy

No production user data exists to migrate (this is a pre-launch portfolio project; existing Sessions are dev/test data created via direct booking with no payment concept at all today — RC4.1's framing of a prior "pay per session" model is aspirational relative to the request, not a description of what is actually implemented, since no payment of any kind exists in the codebase yet). Recommended approach: add `EnrollmentId` to `Session` as **nullable** (a Session created via the existing direct-booking path, if that path is retained at all post-launch of this model, has no Enrollment; a Session created via Enrollment-triggered generation always has one) — this is additive, not a backfill, and requires no data transformation. Whether the existing direct single-session-booking flow (`BookSessionCommandHandler`) is retired entirely once Enrollment-based booking ships, or kept as a parallel path, is itself unresolved by RC4.1's own text (which says "replaces," implying retirement) — Question Requiring Approval, since retiring an existing, tested, working command handler and its route is exactly the kind of public-contract change `CLAUDE.md`'s Stop Conditions name.

## Testing Strategy (outline — no tests written)

- **Domain:** `LearningPlanTests` (soft-validation of cadence, `IsActive` transitions), `EnrollmentTests` (state machine, `RemainingSessions` arithmetic invariant), `EnrollmentPaymentTests` (idempotency, amount immutability — mirroring `ADR-020`'s already-designed `PaymentTests` shape), `RefundRequestTests` (amount-ceiling invariant), `SettlementLedgerEntryTests`/`SettlementBatchTests`.
- **Application:** one test per command handler proving orchestration, authorization (per whatever rows land in `AUTHORIZATION_MATRIX.md`), and validation — per `CLAUDE.md`'s standing rule, using a `FakeUnitOfWork`-equivalent that proves orchestration only, never persistence.
- **Web:** every write re-read from a fresh `DbContext` scope per `CLAUDE.md`'s hard-won rule (the exact defect class `PHASE-01B-REPORT.md`/`PHASE-01C-REPORT.md` already found and fixed elsewhere in this codebase must not be reintroduced here) — most critically for `VerifyEnrollmentPayment` and the Transaction-2 session-generation handler, since both are new, two-transaction flows where a "looks committed but wasn't" bug is especially easy to introduce.
- **End-to-end:** one full flow test using `MockPaymentGateway` — browse plans → initiate enrollment → mock-succeed payment → assert N Sessions exist, each referencing the Enrollment, each booked against a real `AvailabilitySlot`, `Enrollment.Status = Active`, `RemainingSessions` correct — plus one test proving the all-or-nothing rejection when a mid-flight slot race occurs.

## Consequences

**Becomes possible:** a concrete, reviewable design exists for the full commerce model RC4.1 requested, reusing rather than duplicating `ADR-020`'s already-proposed payment-port/idempotency/amount-fixing work.

**Becomes harder / newly constrained:** any implementation phase must resolve four separate scope/design conflicts (Governance Note) before starting, a larger up-front gate than `ADR-020` alone required; `AuditDomainEventHandler`'s `switch` grows by fifteen event types, not five; Enrollment & Billing becomes the first context needing write-triggering invoke access into another context, a new integration shape needing its own scrutiny at implementation time.

**Now forbidden (restated, not new):** no payment gateway SDK, no Stripe dependency (`ADR-018`), no implementation code of any kind under cover of this ADR; no automatic proration/commission formula invented without an explicit owner decision naming one.

## Questions Requiring Approval

1. **Scope — payments.** Does the owner widen v1 scope to include payments (restated from `ADR-020`/`PHASE-05A-REPORT.md` Section 6 Item 1, still unresolved)?
2. **Scope — recurring bookings.** Does the "resolve a weekly-schedule pattern against pre-declared individual slots" design (Cross-Context Integration, above) satisfy `PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md` SCH-4's intent, or does SCH-4 itself need to be revisited/amended given the functional outcome is many Sessions from one purchase?
3. **New bounded context.** Is "Enrollment & Billing" ratified as a fifth bounded context, and is the new Enrollment & Billing → Scheduling & Booking write-invoking edge accepted as an extension to `ADR-002`'s dependency graph?
4. **LearningPlan scope.** Is a Learning Plan a platform-wide catalog entry (matched to any Tutor sharing its Subject), or must it be scoped to specific Tutors?
5. **Insufficient Tutor availability at checkout.** Is "block checkout before payment if not all N required slots exist" the correct behavior, or should partial fulfillment / waitlisting be supported (a materially larger design)?
6. **Session-generation partial failure.** Is "Enrollment Active, generation incomplete, Admin-visible flag for manual resolution" the correct handling for the rare post-payment slot-race failure, or should a different (e.g., auto-refund, auto-retry) policy apply?
7. **Enrollment Cancel/Pause semantics.** What happens to already-generated future Sessions when an Enrollment is Cancelled or Paused — auto-cancel each, or leave them for manual cleanup? Who may invoke Pause (Admin only, or also Tutor/Student)?
8. **Renewal semantics.** Does "Renew Enrollment" create a new `Enrollment`, or extend/reactivate an existing `Completed`/`Expired` one?
9. **Refund proration policy.** Is there a formula (e.g., pro-rated by `RemainingSessions`), or is `ApprovedAmount` always an Admin manual judgment call with no formula?
10. **Settlement per-session amount.** Even split (`PriceAtPurchase / TotalSessions`, zero commission) as recommended, or should a platform commission be deducted — and if so, what rate, and is that itself the `BUSINESS_MODEL.md` Open Question 5 decision being made here or separately?
11. **Existing direct-booking path.** Is `BookSessionCommandHandler`'s current, no-payment, single-session booking flow retired once this model ships, or kept as a parallel path (e.g., for a Tutor's non-plan one-off lessons)?
12. **Audit-trail extension wording.** Are all fifteen new event types audited in full, as recommended, or a narrower subset?
13. **Stripe exclusion.** Confirmed — no objection expected, but recorded as a decision this ADR made on the owner's behalf per authority precedence, not left silent.

## Recommendation / Next Steps

Mirrors `ADR-020`'s own procedural posture: no implementation phase should begin until Questions 1–3 are resolved (they gate whether this can be built at all, and where); Questions 4–11 gate specific Domain/Application details but not the go/no-go decision itself; Questions 12–13 are lower-stakes and can be resolved alongside implementation rather than blocking it.
